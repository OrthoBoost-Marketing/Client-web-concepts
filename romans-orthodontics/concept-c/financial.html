Insurance, in plain words

What orthodontic insurance actually does.

Dental insurance treats orthodontics differently from cleanings and fillings, and the difference is what catches most people out. Here is the honest version, before you are sitting in the chair.

Insurance is a benefit, not a payment plan.

A plan with orthodontic benefits contributes a share of the cost. It does not pay for treatment, and it is not a way to spread payments out. Orthodontic benefits also work against a lifetime orthodontic maximum: a one-time cap on what that plan will ever put toward orthodontics for that person. It behaves differently to an annual dental maximum, which resets every year. Once the lifetime orthodontic maximum is used, it does not come back.

The insurance side is our job, not yours.

Your coverage is verified during the consult itself, which is the point at which it matters, so the price you leave with already accounts for it. You are not sent home to work out your own benefits and report back.

In network, out of network, and what an estimate is.

In network means a practice has agreed set fees with your plan. Out of network means it has not, though many PPO plans still pay toward treatment either way, usually at a lower share. Whichever applies to you, a coverage estimate is an estimate: it is what your plan is expected to pay based on the information available at the time, and it is not a guarantee of payment by your insurer.

No insurance? Here is how the in-house option works.

Plenty of people arrive with no orthodontic benefits at all, and plenty of dental plans simply do not include them. In that case the in-house financing arranged directly with the practice is the usual route, so the cost can be spread out over time rather than met in one go. You still get the same exact written price first, which means you know precisely what you are deciding about before any payment conversation happens.

Varies by plan Whether adults are covered at all, whether there is a waiting period, and the size of the lifetime orthodontic maximum all vary from one plan to the next. That is exactly why your coverage gets verified at the consult instead of guessed at here.

Cost questions

Fair questions about money.

Straight answers, including the one about why there is no price on this page.

Why don't you publish prices on this website?

Because any figure printed here would be wrong for most of the people reading it. What treatment costs depends on your teeth, your bite, how long you will be in treatment and which option you choose. Rather than publish a number that turns into a surprise later, we give you an exact written price for your own case at the free consult.

How much do braces cost in Anthem?

It depends on your case, and we will not guess at it in public. Mild crowding and a complex bite correction are not the same piece of work, so they are not the same price. Your free consult ends with one all-in written figure for your case, treatment and retainers included, and it is yours to keep whether or not you start treatment.

Does dental insurance cover braces for adults?

Sometimes, and it comes down entirely to your plan. Some PPO plans include orthodontic benefits for adults, others limit them to children, and the lifetime orthodontic maximum differs from plan to plan. Bring your plan details to the consult and your actual coverage gets verified rather than estimated. Adult treatment options are set out on the braces for adults page.

Do you offer payment plans?

Yes. We offer in-house financing arranged directly with the practice rather than through a third-party lender, so treatment can be spread out over time. The terms are discussed one to one at your consult, next to your written price, so you can see both at once.

Can I use my FSA or HSA for orthodontic treatment?

Yes, orthodontics is one of the most common uses for both. If you have pre-tax money set aside in a flexible spending or health savings account, you can put it toward treatment here. Bring the details to your consult and we will work it in alongside any insurance coverage.

Is the consultation really free?

Yes, genuinely free, with no obligation attached. It includes a tour, digital scans and imaging, an exam by Dr. Romans, and your exact written price. If you would rather take that number home and think it over, that is a completely normal way for the visit to end.
