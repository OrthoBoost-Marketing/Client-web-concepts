# Romans Orthodontics — what we still need

Concept C is built: 19 pages. Everything below is either blocking launch or
visibly stubbed on the site right now. Grouped by who can answer it, because
that decides how fast it comes back.

---

## A · The office can answer these today

**1. Office hours.** The single most-blocking item. Every page currently shows a
visible `[CONFIRM: office hours]` placeholder, and we have deliberately left
opening hours out of the site's structured data rather than guess them, which
means Google cannot show them. This is the fourth build cycle carrying it.
*Day and time the office opens and closes, per day, plus any lunch closure.*

**2. Is `hello@romansorthodontics.com` correct, and who reads it?** It is
published three times on the contact page. If it is not monitored, we should
publish a different address or none at all.

**3. Which PPO plans do you take?** The financial page currently says "major PPO
plans" and nothing more. Named carriers are one of the highest-value things a
practice page can list, because people search for them by name.

**4. When someone submits a form, how fast does the office call back?** Same
day? Within a business day? The thank-you page and every form currently carry a
`[CONFIRM: callback response time]` placeholder. We will not print a promise we
have not confirmed.

**5. After-hours calls.** We describe it as "calls reach the front desk during
office hours, leave a message outside them and the office calls back." Correct?

**6. In-house financing.** We say the payment agreement is with Romans
Orthodontics rather than an outside lender. **If you route anyone to CareCredit
or a similar company, this sentence is wrong** and needs changing.

**7. Community involvement.** We list Give Kids A Smile, Missions of Mercy,
Dentures for Veterans, and the Arizona Humane Society. Taken from the current
website. Still accurate, anything to add or drop?

**8. Confirm the address detail.** "Ground level, Suite D120, reserved parking
at the door." We also describe the building as being on the south side of the
street, which we read off a map rather than being told.

Right now that page shows visible placeholders where these go. We deliberately
did not invent them, because a made-up origin story on a real doctor's page is
not something we will publish.
- What drew you to orthodontics in the first place.
- Any personal or family experience with dental care that shaped how you treat
  patients. (Skip if there isn't one. It just stays out.)
- Something outside the office: family, hobbies, a Saturday in Anthem.

- soreness for two to three days after braces go on

- the AAO recommendation of a first check around age 7

**11. Two positioning lines we wrote.** Both extend the homepage you approved,
but neither is a direct quote from you:
- "Ceramic brackets are on the table from the first conversation."
- "Adults who were told as teenagers that their window had closed are a routine
  case here."

## C · Your attorney — start this one early, it is the slowest

The privacy, terms, accessibility and refund pages are live as **clearly marked
sample text**. Each one opens with a notice saying it has not been reviewed by
an attorney and has not been adopted by the practice. There are **21 marked
gaps** across them. They are not a launch blocker for design, but they are a
launch blocker for going live.

The two that matter most:

**12. Your HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices.** We have deliberately not
drafted one. It is a regulated document with legally required content and it
has to come from your counsel or your compliance vendor. Please send the
practice's own.

**13. Refund and cancellation terms.** That page is currently a stub. It exists
because your old site had that URL and we do not want the link to break. Since
the site publishes no prices and no treatment terms, there is nothing for us to
write. Send your actual policy, or confirm you are happy for the page to keep
saying that financial arrangements are set out in the signed treatment
agreement.

The rest are governing law and venue, limitation of liability wording, data
retention periods, the analytics and advertising tools in use, a possible COPPA
paragraph, physical accessibility details for the office, and an adoption date
for each page.

## D · Photography — one shoot fixes the most

This is the single highest-leverage thing on the list.

**14. A photo session at the office.** It would resolve four separate problems
at once:
- **The team page is the weakest page on the site.** With one doctor and one
  part-time assistant and no staff photos, it is honest but thin. Real photos
  and written consent to publish turn it into a genuine trust page.
- **Six treatment pages currently use stock photography** of models who read as
  though they work at your practice. Real photos of your office and your
  patients would be a straight upgrade.
- **The stock images we have are low resolution** and look soft on large
  screens. There is no higher-resolution version available.
- **Every photo we have is an iPhone snapshot.** They are usable and we have
  colour-corrected them, but they are not professional photography.

**15. Team roster.** Names, roles, and written consent to appear on the website,
for whoever should be on the team page.
