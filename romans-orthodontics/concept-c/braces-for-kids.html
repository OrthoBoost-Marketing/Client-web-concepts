Questions parents ask

Braces for kids, answered plainly.

How much do braces for kids cost in Anthem?

It depends on the case, so we do not publish a figure. The consultation is free, and it ends with an exact number for your child rather than a range. Major PPO plans are checked while you sit there, and the balance can be financed in house. Two children the same age can need very different treatment lengths.

When should my child first see an orthodontist?

Around age 7. That is when enough permanent teeth are in for a specialist to see how the bite is developing. Most 7-year-olds who come in need nothing yet and leave with a monitoring interval instead of a treatment plan. Waiting until every baby tooth is out can close off the simpler options.

Do braces hurt?

Not the appointment itself. Placing brackets involves no needles and no drilling. Teeth feel sore for two or three days after they are placed and after some adjustments, and soft food plus an over-the-counter pain reliever covers it. If soreness lasts longer than that, call us, because something may need adjusting.

How long will my child be in braces?

Most cases run somewhere between one and two years. Crowding, bite correction and how consistently rubber bands get worn all move that number. Dr. Romans gives you an estimated length at the consultation, before you commit to anything. Missed appointments and broken brackets are the two things that stretch it.

How often are appointments, and will my child miss school?

Adjustments are usually every six to eight weeks and take a short visit rather than a whole afternoon. We schedule on a set rhythm so you can plan around school and work instead of around us. The office is ground level in Suite D120 with reserved parking directly outside.

Do you only treat children?

No. Romans Orthodontics treats kids, teens and adults in the same office, and adults are a routine case here rather than an exception. That is useful for a family, because a parent who decides to treat their own bite keeps the same orthodontist their child already sees. Every first visit is free.
