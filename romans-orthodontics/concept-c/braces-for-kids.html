First braces are a big deal

Nobody gets braces twice.

If you are the kid reading this: yes, it is a big deal. You get to pick your colors, you will feel weird about it for about a week, and then it becomes the least interesting thing about your day.

If you are the parent: you are trying to work out whether now is the right time, who is actually going to do the treatment, and what it will cost before you say yes to anything. Those are the right questions. Nothing on this page asks you to commit to an answer today.

What the first visit feels like

The first visit is a conversation, not a procedure.

You get a tour of the office first, so your child sees the room before anyone sits in a chair. Then imaging and digital scans, which means a small camera moving around the teeth rather than a tray of putty.

Dr. Romans does the exam himself. He looks at the bite and jaw alignment, growth and development, the airway, and the jaw joints, then explains what he found to both of you in plain words. Nothing is placed on your child's teeth that day. You leave with the plan, the exact written price, and time to think about it.

How much do braces for kids cost in Anthem?

It depends on your child's case, and you find out for free before you commit to anything. The consult ends with one exact written all-in price for your child's treatment, retainers included, and it is yours to take home.

We will not quote a number on a web page, because the honest number depends on the exam. If your PPO dental plan applies, we verify it while you are in the office, and FSA or HSA funds work here too. See how insurance and financing work.

When should my child first see an orthodontist?

Around age seven. That is early enough to spot a bite or spacing problem while the jaw is still growing, and early enough that waiting is often the right call.

A first check does not mean braces are coming. Sometimes the answer is to watch and check again in a year. More on early treatment and phase 1.

Do braces hurt?

Putting braces on does not involve needles or drilling. The teeth can feel tender for a few days afterwards as they start to move, and the same is true for a day or so after an adjustment.

If something is bothering your child between visits, call or text (623) 320-1222 rather than waiting it out. Existing patients can get same-day and weekend appointments.

Will my child see Dr. Romans at every visit?

Yes. He is the only orthodontist here and he owns the practice, so there is no rotating provider to hand your child off to.

The person who plans the treatment is the person who adjusts it and finishes it. More about Dr. Romans.

What actually happens at the free consult?

A tour, imaging and digital scans, then an exam by Dr. Romans himself. He assesses the bite and jaw alignment, growth and development, the airway and the jaw joints, then walks you both through what he found and what he would do about it.

You leave with a treatment plan and an exact written all-in price. There is no obligation and no charge for any of it.

What if a bracket breaks or something feels loose?

Call or text (623) 320-1222 and we will get your child seen. Existing patients have access to same-day and weekend appointments for emergencies.

The office is at ground level in Suite D-120 with reserved patient parking outside the door, so a quick fix stays a quick trip.
