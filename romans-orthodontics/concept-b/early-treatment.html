Why you are here

The screening note said see an orthodontist.

So now you are reading a website at 9pm, wondering whether your six year old needs braces. Here is the calm version.

A note from a school screening or from your dentist is a suggestion to get one set of specialist eyes on how your child's jaws and adult teeth are coming in. It is not a diagnosis and it is not a decision. An early orthodontic check is a look, nothing more: Dr. Romans measures how the upper and lower jaws are growing, where the permanent teeth are heading, how the bite fits together and how your child is breathing. Then he tells you what he sees. The reason age 7 comes up at all is that a handful of those things are simpler to guide while a child is still growing.

Parent questions

Early treatment, answered straight.

What age should my child first see an orthodontist?

Around age 7. By then enough permanent teeth have arrived for an orthodontist to see how the bite and the jaws are developing, and it is the age the American Association of Orthodontists names for a first check. Coming in at 7 does not mean starting treatment at 7.

How much does an early orthodontic evaluation cost in Anthem?

It is free. The consult at Romans Orthodontics costs nothing and carries no obligation, and it includes the digital scans, the imaging and the exam with Dr. Romans. If he does recommend treatment, you leave with one exact written all-in price for it, treatment and retainers included, and it is yours to keep.

Will my child definitely need braces later?

An early check cannot promise that either way, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing. What it gives you is a clear picture of what is happening now and what to watch for next. Some children who are treated early still need braces or aligners as teens, so phase 1 is not a way of avoiding that.

Does phase 1 mean two rounds of treatment?

It can. Phase 1 handles something specific while a child is still growing, such as making room for permanent teeth or guiding how the jaws fit together, and a second phase later aligns the full set of adult teeth. Dr. Romans will tell you at the consult whether phase 1 earns its place in your child's case, or whether one round of treatment later is the better plan.

What if we just wait until all the baby teeth are out?

Often that is exactly the recommendation, and choosing to wait is a normal outcome of a first visit. A few situations are simpler to guide while the jaws are still growing, a narrow upper jaw or a crossbite among them, and those are the reasons to look now rather than later. The check is how you find out which situation you are in.

Do we need insurance for an early check?

No, because the consult itself is free. For treatment, Romans Orthodontics accepts major PPO dental plans and verifies your benefits during the visit, and FSA and HSA funds are welcome. One thing worth knowing early: orthodontic coverage runs against a lifetime orthodontic maximum, which is separate from an annual dental maximum. More on insurance and financing.
