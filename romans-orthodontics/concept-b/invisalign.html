What they are

What clear aligners actually are.

Clear aligners are a series of thin, custom-made trays that fit over your teeth. Each tray is shaped slightly differently from the one before it, so wearing them in order moves your teeth a small step at a time. You take them out to eat and to brush, and you move to the next tray on a schedule Dr. Romans sets for your case. They handle crowding, spacing and a lot of bite problems well, and they suit people who will genuinely keep them in for most of the day. If your case needs more force than a tray can apply, or if you already know the trays would sit in a drawer, braces are the better answer and Dr. Romans will say so at the consult.

What to expect

What wearing them is really like.

You keep the trays in for most of the day and night, and take them out to eat, to drink anything other than water, and to brush. A new tray feels tight for the first day or two. That tightness is the tray doing its job, and it settles.

Sometimes a tooth does not follow the tray. That is normal, it is not a failure, and it is exactly why you come in for checks instead of just collecting a box of trays. When something is off track, the usual answer is a fresh scan and a revised set of trays, planned by the same orthodontist who planned the first set. Nothing waits on somebody else's schedule.

Straight answers

Aligner questions we get in Anthem.

How much does Invisalign cost in Anthem?

It depends on your case, and finding out costs nothing. Your free consult ends with one written all-in price for your treatment, retainers included, and it is yours to take home whether or not you start.

There is no figure published here because a small correction and a full case are not the same treatment, and a number on a web page would be a guess about your mouth. Major PPO dental plans are verified during the consult, FSA and HSA funds are accepted, and in-house financing is arranged directly with the practice. See how insurance and financing work.

Are clear aligners as good as braces?

For many cases, yes. For some, no. Aligners handle crowding, spacing and a lot of bite problems well, and they have one real weakness: they only work while they are in your mouth.

Braces do not depend on you remembering, and they can apply force in ways a removable tray cannot, so a complex case can finish better in braces. Dr. Romans plans both, so he has no reason to steer you, and he will tell you at the consult which one actually fits your case. Read about braces for adults.

How many hours a day do I have to wear them?

Most of the day and night. You take the trays out to eat, to drink anything other than water, and to brush, then put them straight back in.

Time out of your mouth is time your teeth are not moving, which makes wear the one part of aligner treatment that is genuinely in your hands. Dr. Romans will be straight with you at the consult about whether that fits your life.

Can my teenager or younger child use clear aligners?

Often, yes. Invisalign First is made for younger children who still have some baby teeth, and Invisalign Teen exists for teenagers, alongside in-house aligners.

Whether aligners are the right call depends on the bite and on how reliably the trays will actually be worn. That is a judgement Dr. Romans makes with you at the consult rather than a rule. See braces for kids or early treatment for the alternatives.

Do you still take putty impressions?

No. Your teeth are captured with a digital scan instead, so there is no tray of impression material and nothing to gag on.

The scan is also what your aligner plan is built from, and it is part of the free consult along with imaging and the exam.

Will I still need a retainer after clear aligners?

Yes. Teeth drift back without retention, whatever moved them in the first place, so retention is part of the treatment rather than an optional extra.

Retainers are inside the written all-in price you get at your consult, so it is not a cost that appears at the end. See the retainer options.
