Why you are here

A jaw that clicks. A child who snores. A bite that never felt right.

These are the things people bring to an orthodontist, and they are worth bringing. An exam can look at how your teeth meet, how the jaw joints move, how wide the upper arch is, how the airway looks on imaging, and whether the teeth show the wear that comes with clenching.

Here is the part a lot of websites leave out. None of that is a diagnosis of sleep apnea, and no orthodontist can give you one. Sleep-disordered breathing is diagnosed by a physician, usually with a sleep study. Orthodontic treatment is not a cure for sleep apnea, and it does not treat ADHD, bedwetting, or anything else you may have read in a forum. What an orthodontist can honestly do is examine, describe what is there, treat the part that is orthodontic, and refer the rest.

What the first conversation is like

Sometimes the right answer is a referral, and you will hear it.

You start with a free consult. Dr. Romans looks at the bite and the jaw joints, takes digital scans and imaging, asks what you have noticed and for how long, then tells you what he sees in plain words.

Straight answers

How much does airway or TMJ treatment cost in Anthem?

It depends on what the examination actually finds, so the visit that answers it is free. Your consult ends with one exact written price for your case, covering treatment and retainers, and it is yours to keep whether or not you start.

Major PPO dental plans are accepted and verified during the consult, and FSA and HSA funds are welcome. Orthodontic benefits work against a lifetime orthodontic maximum, which is not the same thing as an annual dental maximum, and adult coverage varies by plan. See how insurance and financing work.

Can braces or an expander cure sleep apnea?

No. Sleep apnea is a medical diagnosis, made by a physician and usually confirmed with a sleep study, and orthodontic treatment is not a cure for it.

What orthodontics can address is structural: a narrow upper arch, crowding, or a jaw position. Some patients are also fitted with an oral appliance as one part of a plan their physician is directing. Dr. Romans will not tell you that you have sleep apnea, and he will not tell you that braces will fix it.

My child snores. Should I bring them in?

Bring them in, and tell their pediatrician too. Both conversations matter, and only the physician's side of it can lead to a diagnosis.

An orthodontic exam can tell you whether the upper arch is narrow, whether there is enough room for the teeth coming in, and how the bite is developing. That is useful information, and it is not a diagnosis. If what he sees points toward tonsils, adenoids or allergies, Dr. Romans will say so and refer. Read how early treatment works.

Will a TMJ appliance stop my jaw pain?

We cannot promise that, and it is worth being careful with anyone who does. Jaw joint pain has more than one possible source, and not all of them are orthodontic.

An appliance is one tool. It can change how the teeth come together and how much work the jaw muscles are asked to do. Some people notice a difference, and some do not. Dr. Romans will tell you what he thinks an appliance can and cannot change before you commit to one, and he will refer you on if the picture looks like something other than a bite problem.

Do I need a sleep study before you can make me a sleep appliance?

Yes, that is the correct order. The diagnosis comes from a physician, and a sleep appliance or mandibular advancement appliance is provided as part of the care they are directing.

If you have not had a sleep study, the consult is still worth having. An exam and imaging tell you what is going on structurally, and Dr. Romans can help you get in front of the right physician for the rest.

Is the airway and TMJ check extra, or part of a normal consult?

It is part of every consult, for every patient, at no charge. Nobody has to ask for it.

The free consult includes a tour, digital scans and imaging instead of impressions, an exam by Dr. Romans, a bite and jaw alignment assessment, a growth and development evaluation, an airway assessment, a TMJ evaluation, and a written treatment plan with your exact price.
