Sec. 01
The path
With a patient in the treatment area

Why orthodontics?

The pattern in his path.

You can learn a lot about a doctor from the choices he made before anyone was watching. Alongside his dental degree at A.T. Still University, Dr. Romans earned a certificate in public health: extra study, chosen, in how care reaches whole communities. After his orthodontic specialty training, he completed a craniofacial fellowship with the team at St. Louis Children's Hospital, treating children with cleft lip and palate. Some of the most complex, highest-stakes cases in the field.

And through it all, he kept showing up to volunteer clinics: Give Kids A Smile, Missions of Mercy, Dentures for Veterans. The pattern is hard to miss: at each fork, he picked the version of dentistry that was about people, not just teeth. Orthodontics, done one patient at a time in his own practice, is where that path led: more than seven years dedicated to orthodontics, including advanced specialty training.

How he practices.

Plans he makes. Checks he does himself.
